<span class="style2">
<p></p><u>INFORMATION</u></span><span class="style6">
<p></p>
</span>
<p align="left"><img align="right" height="254" hspace="5" src="barstow.jpg" title="Dick (left) underground in old workings on the south lond 130fm level Levant Mine with Gary Reynolds (centre) and John Gorvin (right)" width="301"/><span class="style2">Richard  William Barstow (1947–1982), known universally as Dick, was born in  Bangor, north Wales on 7th January 1947. He was brought up in  Caernarvonshire, and was later educated at Selsdon Grammar School,  Dick joined an insurance company, but the job didn’t suit him and  he left to look for work in Cornwall during the mining revival of the  1960s. He worked for a time in the sampling office of South Crofty  mine, and subsequently in the same capacity at Geevor mine.</span></p>
<p align="left" class="style2">He was the most enterprising of the modern-day field collectors in  Britain, and a formidable mineralogist. Trading by mail order from  his home in Tregeseal, near Botallack, Cornwall, he began his mineral  business in December 1972, later moving to Drakewalls House,  Gunnislake in 1978. </p>
<p align="left" class="style2">Energetic and thorough, in everything that he did, he researched and visited  many classic and recent localities and was remarkably successful in  recovering good specimens. His hall-mark green card labels are still  to be seen at mineral shows <a href="http://www.mindat.org/photosearch.php?frm_id=mls&amp;cform_is_valid=1&amp;minname=&amp;cf_mls_page=1&amp;region=&amp;text=richard+barstow&amp;otype=0&amp;stype=0&amp;phototype=M&amp;mtype=0&amp;sort=&amp;submit_mls=Search" target="_blank">and on the Internet</a>, together with the  less-common white labels which accompany specimens from his personal  collection. </p>
<p align="left" class="style2">His monthly lists were very good, always containing something affordable  and worth having, and his descriptions very accurate. </p>
<p align="left" class="style2">Whilst not an academic, or scientifically educated collector, Dick was  extremely knowledgeable and highly respected within mineralogical  circles. Sadly he died at the young age of 35, from liver cancer. In  1986 the Plymouth City Museum &amp; Art Gallery purchased a portion  of his fine private collection of Cornish and Devonshire minerals.</p>
<p align="left" class="style2">This archive contains a more-or less complete set of his monthly lists.  Some of the lists are in poor condition, and others have not  reproduced well. If you are able to provide better scanned copies of  any of the lists, please contact the webmaster. I would like to  acknowledge help received from Richard Hotston, Nick Sleep, and Nick  Carruth who assisted in this project by lending copies of lists not  in my possession.</p>
<p align="left" class="style2">Roy Starkey<br/>
</p>
<p align="left" class="style2">March  2010</p>
<p align="left" class="style2">For  additional information about Dick see: <em>Starkey, R.E., &amp; Cooper,  M.P. (2010) Richard W. Barstow - Mineral Dealer Extraordinaire.  UK  Journal of Mines &amp; Minerals <strong>31</strong>,  7-57</em>.</p>
