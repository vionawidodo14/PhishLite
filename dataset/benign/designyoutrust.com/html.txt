<h1 id="post-952712"><a href="https://designyoutrust.com/2019/10/nikon-small-world-2019-the-best-microscope-photos-of-the-year/">Nikon Small World 2019: The Best Microscope Photos Of The Year</a></h1>
<div style="height:15px;"></div>
<div style="clear:both;"></div>
<div class="mainpost">
<p>Nikon has announced the winners of the 2019 Small World Photomicrography Competition, and has once more shared some of the winning and honored images with us. The contest invites photographers and scientists to submit images of all things visible under a microscope. This year, first place was awarded to Teresa Zgoda and Teresa Kugler for their painstakingly prepared photo of a turtle embryo, using fluorescence and stereo microscopy. More than 2,000 entries were received from 100 countries in 2019, the 45th year of the competition.</p>

<p><strong>1st Place:</strong> Teresa Zgoda &amp; Teresa Kugler, Campbell Hall, New York, USA. Fluorescent turtle embryo. Stereomicroscopy, Fluorescence, 5x (Objective Lens Magnification). <h1 id="post-952515"><a href="https://designyoutrust.com/2019/10/11-stunning-colorized-photos-showing-the-street-life-of-victorian-london-from-over-140-years-ago/">11 Stunning Colorized Photos Showing The Street Life Of Victorian London From Over 140 Years Ago</a></h1>
<div style="height:15px;"></div>
<div style="clear:both;"></div>
<div class="mainpost">
<p>According to Tom Marshall, a professional photo colouriser: “n the mid-1870s, Scottish photographer John Thomson captured the daily toil and struggle of the ‘street folks’ of London, in a series of photos that laid the foundations for modern photojournalism. Working with a radical journalist called Adolphe Smith, Thomson produced a monthly magazine ‘Street Life in London’ from 1876 to 1877.</p>
<p>The photographs Thomson took depict real life in London, showing the poorest of the poor and how they managed to survive, in scenes that could have been written by Charles Dickens. Smith would interview the subjects of the photos, often preserving the unique dialects and expressions of a world now long forgotten, and the photos lent authenticity to his text. Thomson and Smith published their photos and interviews in a book in 1878 from which the following images were taken.</p>
<p>I believe that colourizing images can allow a modern audience to engage better with the subject, especially in an age where we see thousands of images on a news feed every day. Colour brings out hidden details, which are often lost in black and white, and it causes the viewer to pause and look. This is not to say that the original images are not fascinating in their own right, but I believe that the addition of colour helps to enhance the scene and forces the viewer to spend more time looking into it and reading the accompanying caption.”</p>
<p><a href="https://designyoutrust.com/2019/10/11-stunning-colorized-photos-showing-the-street-life-of-victorian-london-from-over-140-years-ago/" title="11 Stunning Colorized Photos Showing The Street Life Of Victorian London From Over 140 Years Ago"><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-952522" height="887" sizes="(max-width: 700px) 100vw, 700px" src="https://main-designyoutrust.netdna-ssl.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/1-63.jpg?iv=100" srcset="https://main-designyoutrust.netdna-ssl.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/1-63.jpg?iv=100 700w, https://main-designyoutrust.netdna-ssl.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/1-63-650x824.jpg?iv=100 650w" width="700"/></a></p>
<p>“There are, undoubtedly, many most honest, hard-working, and in every sense worthy men, who hold licenses from the Watermen’s Company, or from the Thames Conservancy.
