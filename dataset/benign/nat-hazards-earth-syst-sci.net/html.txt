<div class="authors">Manuela I. Brunner, Katharina Liechti, and Massimiliano Zappa</div>
<div class="citation">Nat. Hazards Earth Syst. Sci., 19, 2311–2323, <nobr class="hide-on-mobile hide-on-tablet">https://doi.org/10.5194/nhess-19-2311-2019,</nobr><span class="hide-on-desktop">https://doi.org/10.5194/nhess-19-2311-2019,</span> 2019</div>

The return period estimates depended on the region, variable, and return period considered.

<a class="article-title" href="https://www.nat-hazards-earth-syst-sci.net/19/2295/2019/" target="_parent">Ensemble models from machine learning: an example  of wave runup and coastal dune erosion</a>
<div class="authors">Tomas Beuzen, Evan B. Goldstein, and Kristen D. Splinter</div>
<div class="citation">Nat. Hazards Earth Syst. Sci., 19, 2295–2309, <nobr class="hide-on-mobile hide-on-tablet">https://doi.org/10.5194/nhess-19-2295-2019,</nobr><span class="hide-on-desktop">https://doi.org/10.5194/nhess-19-2295-2019,</span> 2019</div>
<span class="show-hide journal-contentLinkColor triangle short_summary_button_75213" data-hide=".short_summary_button_75213" data-show=".short_summary_75213">Short summary</span>
<div class="j-widget__max short_summary short_summary_75213" style="display: none">
<div class="widget dark-border">
<div class="legend journal-contentLinkColor">Short summary</div>
<div class="content">
                                Wave runup is important for characterizing coastal vulnerability to wave action; however, it is complex and uncertain to predict. We use machine learning with a high-resolution dataset of wave runup to develop an accurate runup predictor that includes prediction uncertainty. We show how uncertainty in wave runup predictions can be used practically in a model of dune erosion to make ensemble predictions that provide more information and greater predictive skill than a single deterministic model.

<div class="authors">Xian Luo, Xuemei Fan, Yungang Li, and Xuan Ji</div>
<div class="citation">Nat. Hazards Earth Syst. Sci. Discuss., <nobr class="hide-on-mobile hide-on-tablet">https://doi.org/10.5194/nhess-2019-327,</nobr><span class="hide-on-desktop">https://doi.org/10.5194/nhess-2019-327,</span> 2019</div>

<span class="show-hide journal-contentLinkColor triangle short_summary_button_80707" data-hide=".short_summary_button_80707" data-show=".short_summary_80707">Short summary</span>
<div class="j-widget__max short_summary short_summary_80707" style="display: none">
<div class="widget dark-border">
<div class="legend journal-contentLinkColor">Short summary</div>
<div class="content">
                                In this study, we corrected Asian Precipitation Highly Resolved Observational Data Integration Towards Evaluation of Water Resources (APHRODITE) in the Yarlung Tsangpo-Brahmaputra River Basin using both linear and nonlinear methods, and their influences on resulting extreme precipitation analysis were assessed. Results showed that all methods were able to correct mean precipitation, but their ability to correct wet-day frequency and coefficient of variation were markedly different.

<a class="article-title" href="https://www.nat-hazards-earth-syst-sci.net/19/2281/2019/" target="_parent">Identifying a transition climate zone in an arid river basin using the evaporative stress index</a>
<div class="authors">Yongqiang Liu, Lu Hao, Decheng Zhou, Cen Pan, Peilong Liu, Zhe Xiong, and Ge Sun</div>
<div class="citation">Nat. Hazards Earth Syst. Sci., 19, 2281–2294, <nobr class="hide-on-mobile hide-on-tablet">https://doi.org/10.5194/nhess-19-2281-2019,</nobr><span class="hide-on-desktop">https://doi.org/10.5194/nhess-19-2281-2019,</span> 2019</div>
<span class="show-hide journal-contentLinkColor triangle short_summary_button_61323" data-hide=".short_summary_button_61323" data-show=".short_summary_61323">Short summary</span>
<div class="j-widget__max short_summary short_summary_61323" style="display: none">
<div class="widget dark-border">
<div class="legend journal-contentLinkColor">Short summary</div>
<div class="content">
                                A transition zone often exists between a moist upper river reach and an arid lower reach in a watershed with complex topography. This zone is more suitable for human activities but is difficult to identify in climate classification. We found that a hydrological index overpowers a meteorological index in identifying a transition zone of a watershed in northwestern China, indicating the important role of the land-surface processes and human disturbances in formulating the transition zone.

<a class="article-title" href="https://www.nat-hazards-earth-syst-sci-discuss.net/nhess-2019-263/" target="_parent">Investigating beach erosion related with its recovery at Phra Thong Island, Thailand caused by the 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami</a>
<div class="authors">Ryota Masaya, Anawat Suppasri, Kei Yamashita, Fumihiko Imamura, Chris Gouramanis, and Natt Leelawat</div>

<div class="legend journal-contentLinkColor">Short summary</div>
<div class="content">
                                This study examines the sediment transport during the 2004 Indian Ocean Tsunami event on Phra Thong Island, Thailand. We use numerical simulations and sediment transportation models, and our modelling approach confirms that the beaches were significantly eroded predominantly during the first backwash phase. Although 2004 tsunami deposits are found on the island, we demonstrate that most of the sediment was deposited in the shallow coastal area, facilitating quick recovery of the beach.
