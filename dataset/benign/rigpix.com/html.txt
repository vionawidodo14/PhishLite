<h5>The amateur's code, formulated in 1928</h5>
</font></font></font></center><font size="2"><font size="1"><font size="2">
<font size="1">
<strong>§1 - The amateur is considerate</strong><br/>
He never knowingly uses the air in such a way as to lessen the pleasure of others<br/>
<br/>
<strong>§2 - The amateur is loyal</strong><br/>
He offers his loyalty, encouragement and support to his fellow radio amateurs, his local club and his national association, through which amateur radio is represented<br/>
<br/>
<strong>§3 - The amateur is progressive</strong><br/>
He keeps his station abreast of science. It is well-built and efficient. His operating practice is above reproach<br/>
<br/>
<strong>§4 - The amateur is friendly</strong><br/>
Slow and patient sending when requested, friendly advice and counsel to the beginner, kindly assistance, cooperation and consideration for the interests of others; these are the marks of the amateur spirit<br/>
<br/>
<strong>§5 - The amateur is balanced</strong><br/>
Radio is his hobby. He never allows it to interfere with any of the duties he owes to his home, his job, his school or his community<br/>
<br/>
<strong>§6 - The amateur is patriotic</strong><br/>
His knowledge and his station are always ready for service of his country and his community<br/>
