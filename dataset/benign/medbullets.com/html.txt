<h5 class="section-description__header">SKILL PREPARATION TASKS</h5>
<p>
                         You can't expect to do any surgical skill, for instance cutting the femoral neck in a THA,
                         unless you have done your homework. Prepare for surgical skills by reading the basic outline of the skill steps
                         watching select videos, and reading key articles and portions of textbook chapters.
                         Take notes and highlight so you don't forget what you learned.
                    </p>
<p>
<strong>Target Content:</strong>
                         Medbullets has carefully created a series of tasks that we believe a resident should complete in preparation for a skill.
                         They include: <br/>
                         1) STEPS - reading the Medbullets "Steps" of a skill that have been created by medbullets.
                         Medbullets Techniques are largerly incomplete at this time, and will see rapid improvement
                         as they are updated by experts in the field over the coming months.
                    </p>
<p>
<strong>Mastery Trigger:</strong>
                         Check the "Mark Skill as Read" under each Step. <br/>
                         2) VIDEOS - only Medbullets Technique Videos count. Currently we only have videos for one procedure posted.
                         We plan on releasing 1-2 Technique Videos per month.
                    </p>
<p>
<strong>Mastery Trigger:</strong>
                         Click on Video Selfmastery Tool of Skill Technique Video per the scale listed above under videos. <br/>
                         3) ARTICLES - we will continue to select several articles, which may be a scientific articles or a section
                         of a Technique textbook, which we feel should be read prior to attempting to do a surgical skill.

<h5 class="section-description__header">FACULTY ENGAGEMENT &amp; FEEDBACK</h5>
<p> Just like you need a "spotter" when benching 20lbs more than ever before, you need to engage faculty
                     so they can "spot" you on your "sweet spot" skills - the skills you can't quite master on your own.
                     By focusing faculty teaching effort on your target "sweet spot" skills, we believe we can greatly accelerate
                      how you move up the learning curve.
                    </p>
<p>
<strong>Target Content:</strong>
                         A "sweet spot" skill is one in which the resident has completed all the prerequisite skills
                         (skill of easier complexity level), has completed on the Preparatory Tasks, and has Self-Mastered to 80%.
                         In our opinion, only then is a resident ready to engage a faculy and have the most productive teaching/learning experience in the OR.
                     </p>
<p>
<strong>Mastery Trigger:</strong>
                         Faculty MSE Level increase once faculty or resident assess you as a good or excellent on MSE. <br/>
                         SKILL COMPLEXITY LEVEL: Our surgical Skills, are broken down into 5 levels of complexity and
                         correlate with the ACGME milestone levels. A resident should reach a Level 4 by the time he graduates from residency.
                         He should have reached a Level 5 by the time he has completed his fellowship.
                     </p>
