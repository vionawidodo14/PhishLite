<div class="entry-content copy">
<p><span style="color: #000000;"><span style="color: #e3314f;"><strong>Opportunity Contact Role</strong></span> has two aspects to it: (1) it represents the role a Contact play on an Opportunity; and, (2) it is a junction object between Opportunity and Contact. As we all know, <span style="color: #000080;"><strong>Opportunity</strong></span> and <span style="color: #800080;"><strong>Contact</strong></span> share a <span style="color: #d41e11;"><strong>many-to-many relationship</strong></span>. For example, a Contact can play a Decision maker role in one Opportunity; and, an Executive sponsor role in another opportunity.</span></p>
<p><span style="color: #000000;">The challenge arises when one wants to configure automation around the Opportunity object and Contact Roles. For example, let’s say that – (1) whenever someone creates an opportunity for an Account, you want to auto add <em>all contacts</em> as contact roles; or, (2) whenever someone creates an opportunity for an Account, you want to auto add <em>key contacts</em> as contact role for the Opportunity.</span></p>
<p><span style="color: #000000;">How would you handle such a requirement? Well, let us take a business use case and try to understand how one can automate such processes.</span></p>
<h1>Business Use case</h1>
<p><strong><span style="color: #000000;">Martin </span>Jones</strong><span style="color: #000000;"> is is a System Administrator at Gurukul on Cloud (GoC). He is in the process of implementing Opportunity management at GoC. Last week, he received the following request from Pardot marketing team:</span></p>
<ul>
<li><span style="color: #000000;">Whenever Opportunities are created for Partner Account, automatically add contacts, with Chief Executive officer in their Title, as a primary contact role on the Opportunities.</span></li>
</ul>
