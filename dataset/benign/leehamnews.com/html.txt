<h2 class="headline small"><a href="https://leehamnews.com/2019/10/21/nma-off-the-table-in-2020-and-maybe-entirely/" rel="bookmark" title="NMA off the table in 2020–and maybe entirely">NMA off the table in 2020–and maybe entirely</a></h2>
<p><strong>Subscription Required</strong></p>
<h5><strong>Introduction</strong></h5>
<p><strong>Oct. 21, 2019, © Leeham News, New York</strong>: What is the impact of the 737 MAX grounding on Boeing’s plan for the New Midmarket Airplane (NMA)?<a href="https://leehamnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/N797LH-protected.png"><img alt="" class="alignleft size-full wp-image-30019" src="https://leehamnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/N797LH-protected.png"/></a></p>
<p>This question was common along the sidelines last week of the Wings Club and two conferences in New York City. (See Pontifications.)</p>
<p>There is, of course, no definitive answer today.</p>
<p>But the plurality of opinion is that the NMA is off the table for the indefinite future.</p>
<h5><strong>Summary</strong></h5>
<ul>
<li>There is no clear picture when the MAX will be recertified, either by the FAA or other regulators.</li>
<li>There is no clear picture how long Boeing will maintain production rate at 42/mo, which depends on how much longer it must wait for recertification.</li>
<li>With turmoil in the executive suite (the CEO lost his chairman’s title Oct. 11), there is no clear picture how long Dennis Muilenburg has a job.</li>
<li>With, apparently, Boeing Commercial CEO Kevin McAllister also in the cross hairs, there is no clear picture of executive suite stability at Longacres, the BCA HQ.</li>
<li>Any successors for Muilenburg and/or McAllister will want to review Boeing’s entire product strategy.</li>
<li>Therefore, do not expect any go-forward for NMA in 2020.</li>
</ul>
<p>Other than that, everything is fine.</p>

<p><strong>Subscription Required</strong></p>
<p><strong>By Vincent Valery</strong></p>
<p><strong>Introduction   </strong></p>
<p><strong>Oct. 21, 2019, © Leeham News:</strong> As Boeing sorts out final requirements with regulators for the 737 MAX return to service, preparations to resume deliveries are in full steam.</p>
<p>The company is hiring scores of temporary workers to return grounded and built but not yet delivered airframes. A note from Alliance Bernstein estimates that Boeing will be able to hand over 25 aircraft per month on top of those that come off the assembly line.</p>
<p>After taking hefty losses and having lost its most robust cash flow source for almost a year, Boeing will want to hand over as many aircraft to airlines as fast as possible.</p>
<p>Do all 737 MAX customers, likewise, want their aircraft back in service as soon as possible?</p>

<ul>
<li>National regulators will drive return to service timeline;</li>
<li>Passenger demand variations;</li>
<li>737 MAX exposure by region;</li>
<li>Demand peaks might dictate who flies first;</li>
<li>Maintenance, compensation, and other considerations.</li>

<p>By Bjorn Fehrm</p>
<p><strong>October 17, 2019, ©. Leeham News:</strong> Over the last decades the focus for new airliner projects has been technological advancements in aerodynamics, structures, engines, and avionics. While still important, the next airliner projects have an additional focus which has moved to the top of the list. The production phase and how to improve its many parts.</p>

<p><strong>Subscription Required</strong></p>
<h5><strong>Introduction</strong></h5>
<p><strong>October 17, 2019, © Leeham News:</strong> FlightGlobal writes Boeing is investigating re-engining the 767-400ER with GE GEnx engines to produce a new freighter and perhaps a passenger aircraft as a replacement for the NMA project. Development costs would be lower and it would be easier to get a business plan which closes for the upgraded 767 than for the NMA.</p>
<p>We commented on the idea <a href="https://leehamnews.com/2019/10/14/pontifications-the-time-for-a-767re-passenger-model-has-come-and-gone/">earlier in the week</a> and here follows a technical analysis of what re-engining the 767 would bring.</p>
<p><a href="https://leehamnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/11/n672ua-united-airlines-boeing-767-322erw.jpg"><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-25548" src="https://leehamnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/11/n672ua-united-airlines-boeing-767-322erw.jpg"/></a></p>
<p><strong>Summary:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>The 767 is 40 years old in its base design. We look at the fundamentals to understand the trades involved in extending its life with new engines.</li>
<li>We also compare the 767 technologies with those for the NMA to understand the compromises of an updated 767RE compared with a clean sheet NMA.</li>
</ul>

<p></p><div class="wp-caption alignleft" id="attachment_29701" style="width: 107px"><a href="https://leehamnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/Hamilton-5.jpg"><img alt="" aria-describedby="caption-attachment-29701" class="wp-image-29701" height="142" src="https://leehamnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/Hamilton-5.jpg" width="97"/></a><p class="wp-caption-text" id="caption-attachment-29701">By Scott Hamilton</p></div><p></p>

Some believe Friday’s action by the Boeing Board of Directors “stripping” (as most media headlines and stories positioned it) the chairman’s title from Muilenburg, while his retaining the president and CEO titles, is the first step in easing him out the door next year. This is my view, too.</p>

<p>Others think handing the non-executive chairman’s title to lead director David Calhoun is actually an effort to save Muilenburg’s job.</p>
<p>Here’s the divergent thinking. None of the analysts wanted to be identified because by investment bank policy, their remarks hadn’t been cleared for quotation and none had yet issued research notes in reaction.</p>

<h2 class="headline small"><a href="https://leehamnews.com/2019/10/14/pontifications-the-time-for-a-767re-passenger-model-has-come-and-gone/" rel="bookmark" title="The time for a 767RE passenger model has come and gone">The time for a 767RE passenger model has come and gone</a></h2>
<p><strong>Subscription Required</strong></p>
<h5><strong>Introduction</strong></h5>
<p><strong>Oct. 14, 2019, © Leeham News</strong>: Boeing is reconsidering a previous idea to re-engine the 767 with GEnx powerplants, <em>Flight Global</em> <a href="https://www.flightglobal.com/news/articles/boeing-examines-genx-powered-767-x-for-cargo-and-pa-461386/">reported last week</a>.</p>
<p>The idea was run up the flagpole, so-to-speak, in 2017.</p>
<p></p><div class="wp-caption alignleft" id="attachment_31369" style="width: 1268px"><a href="https://leehamnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/DL767.png"><img alt="" aria-describedby="caption-attachment-31369" class="wp-image-31369 size-full" height="382" src="https://leehamnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/DL767.png" width="1258"/></a><p class="wp-caption-text" id="caption-attachment-31369">Boeing 747-400. Source: Delta Air Lines.</p></div><p></p>
<p>At that time, the 737 MAX was just entering service. There was, of course, no hint of any turbulence on the horizon.</p>
<p>The business case for the New Midmarket Aircraft was difficult even then. So why not look at a 767RE and restarting the 757 line, also up upgrades?</p>
<p>Boeing being Boeing, it looks at everything. It ruled out restarting the 757 line (the challenges would have been pretty daunting).</p>
<p>The 767 got more studious traction, including simply restarting the passenger line and providing a really cheap acquisition. A 767RE, however, was viewed as too complex under the circumstances and it would compete with the 787.</p>
<p>American Airlines and United Airlines were actually interested in the airplane restart.</p>
<h5><strong>Summary</strong></h5>
<ul>
<li>B767F-RE may be to counter A330-900F approach to a major cargo airline.</li>
<li>B767P-RE likely would be met favorably–by Airbus.</li>
<li>Re-engining 767 repeats 737RE experience.</li>
</ul>
