<h2 style="text-align: left;">Privacy Tools &amp; Solutions</h2>
<p style="text-align: left;">At this very moment you are being <strong>tracked</strong>, <strong>monitored</strong>, and <strong>recorded</strong> by various government and private entities around the world. Private companies, such as Google and Facebook, are gathering as much of your data as possible to hit you with targeted ads and influence your purchasing decisions (behavioral modification). Government entities, such as the NSA and GCHQ surveillance agencies, are <strong>bulk-collecting your private data</strong> for various purposes.</p>
<p style="text-align: left;">You live in a world of total surveillance that is carried out with the close cooperation between private companies and state agencies – see the <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PRISM_(surveillance_program)" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">PRISM program</a> for details.</p>
<p style="text-align: left;">Fortunately, there are <strong>effective solutions</strong> to these problems that you can begin implementing <strong>right now</strong>.</p>
<div class="shadow-spacer">
<div class="mask"></div>
</div>
<h3 style="text-align: left;">WEB BROWSER</h3>
<p style="text-align: left;">Using a secure browser that protects your privacy is absolutely crucial to stay safe and secure online. Your browser may contain vast amounts of private information, such as all the websites you visit (browsing history), login credentials, personal information (autofill details), as well as system data and location information. This information can be exploited by hackers or simply collected by the browser itself – such as with Google Chrome, Opera, or Microsoft Edge.</p>
<p style="text-align: left;"><a href="https://www.mozilla.org/en-US/firefox/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank"><strong>Firefox</strong></a> is a great browser that can be configured for more privacy and security – see the <a href="https://restoreprivacy.com/firefox-privacy/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Firefox privacy</a> guide. Other good browsers include <a href="https://brave.com/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Brave</a> and <a href="https://www.waterfoxproject.org/en-US/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Waterfox</a>, which is a fork of Firefox that is more privacy-friendly. More browsers are also discussed in the <a href="https://restoreprivacy.com/secure-browser/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">secure browser</a> information guide.</p>
<p><img alt="secure browser restore privacy" class="aligncenter wp-image-32650" height="251" src="https://cdn-resprivacy.pressidium.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/secure-browser-restore-privacy-.jpg" width="400"/></p>
<p style="text-align: left;"><strong>Browser fingerprinting</strong> – It’s worth noting that your browser and operating system’s unique preferences and settings can also be used to track and identify you, which is called browser fingerprinting. To mitigate this risk, see the <a href="https://restoreprivacy.com/browser-fingerprinting/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">browser fingerprinting</a> guide.</p>
<p style="text-align: left;"><strong>WebRTC leak</strong> – The <a href="https://restoreprivacy.com/webrtc-leaks/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">WebRTC leak</a> is a vulnerability that can expose your real IP address through WebRTC stun requests, even when you are using a good VPN service. Check out the <a href="https://restoreprivacy.com/webrtc-leaks/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">WebRTC leak</a> guide to remedy this issue.</p>
<p style="text-align: left;"><strong>Useful browser add-ons</strong> – Depending on your unique circumstances, you may find some of these browser add-ons to be useful:</p>
<ol style="text-align: left;">
<li><strong>uBlock Origin</strong> – A powerful blocker for advertisements and tracking.</li>
<li><strong>HTTPS Everywhere</strong> – This forces an HTTPS connection with the sites you visit.</li>
<li><strong>Cookie AutoDelete</strong> – Deletes those unwanted tracking cookies.</li>
<li><strong>Privacy Badger</strong> – Another add-on from the Electronic Frontier Foundation, Privacy Badger blocks spying ads and trackers.</li>
<li><strong>uMatrix</strong> – While this may be overkill for many users, uMatrix is a powerful add-on that gives you control over requests that may be tracking you on various websites. (Advanced users – requires lots of customization.)</li>
<li><strong>NoScript</strong> – This is a script blocker that allows you to control which scripts run on the sites you visit. (Advanced users – requires lots of customization.)</li>
</ol>
<p style="text-align: left;">Pairing a good VPN service with a secure and private browser will go a long way to restoring your privacy.</p>
<div class="shadow-spacer">
<div class="mask"></div>
</div>
<h3 style="text-align: left;"><strong>VPN (VIRTUAL PRIVATE NETWORK)</strong></h3>
<p style="text-align: left;">Whenever you go online without a VPN, you broadcast to the world your <strong>unique IP address</strong>, internet provider, and geographic location. This private information is being tracked/recorded by the websites you visit, advertising networks, government agencies, and other third parties. Every device that connects to the internet has a unique IP address, which enables third parties to track and identify you online. By default, you do not have online privacy.</p>
<p style="text-align: left;"><strong>ISP Spying</strong>: Throughout most of the world, internet service providers (ISPs) are tracking and recording the browsing history of their customers. In the United States, Canada, Australia, the UK, and much of Europe, this data is provided to government agencies, where it may be stored for years (data retention laws). Since March of 2017, US internet providers can also sell the private data they collect to third parties (such as advertisers).</p>
<p style="text-align: left;"><strong>Solution</strong>: A VPN (virtual private network) will restore your privacy be creating an encrypted tunnel between your device and a VPN server. This makes your online activities unreadable to third parties (including your ISP), since your traffic is getting encrypted and anonymized by the VPN. Additionally, a VPN replaces your real IP address and location with that of the VPN server you are using. A VPN is also a useful tool for getting around content restrictions and accessing blocked websites. While this may sound complex, all you need to do is install a VPN app on your device and connect to one of the VPN servers – (see the <a href="https://restoreprivacy.com/what-is-vpn/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">VPNs explained</a> guide).</p>

<h3 style="text-align: left;">AD BLOCKER</h3>
<p style="text-align: left;">Many ads function as advanced tracking, quietly recording your browsing history and online habits. The more data ad companies can collect about you, the more money they can make with targeted advertising. Blocking ads will improve your privacy and also page load speeds.</p>
<p style="text-align: left;">Ads can also be malicious (malvertising) and therefore a security risk. Because ads are fed in through third-party domains that can be hijacked, they are an easy attack vector and can infect your device as you load a website – no clicks required.</p>
<p><img alt="ad blocker" class="aligncenter wp-image-32657" height="265" src="https://cdn-resprivacy.pressidium.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/ad-blocker-.png" width="400"/></p>
<p style="text-align: left;">Here are a few different ways to block ads:</p>
<ol>
<li style="text-align: left;"><strong>Browser add-ons</strong> – These work as extensions through your internet browser. One popular option is <a href="https://github.com/gorhill/uBlock" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">uBlock Origin</a>. The drawback however, is that these only work with supported browsers.</li>
<li style="text-align: left;"><strong>VPN ad blockers</strong> – You can also block ads through a VPN service. There are a handful of VPNs that offer ad blocking – check out the <a href="https://restoreprivacy.com/vpn-ad-blocker-comparison/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">VPN ad blocker</a> guide for more information.</li>
<li style="text-align: left;"><strong>Hardware</strong> – Some routers have the option to block ads. Other hardware options include the <a href="https://restoreprivacy.com/go/eblocker" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">eBlocker</a> device, or running a Raspberry Pi with Pi-Hole.</li>
</ol>

<h3 style="text-align: left;">PRIVATE SEARCH ENGINE</h3>
<p style="text-align: left;">The major search engines, such as Google, Bing, and Yahoo, are collecting your search requests, IP address, user agent, and also tracking you through cookies. Your searches allow these companies to build up intimate user profiles, which can be handed over to various third parties.</p>
<p><img alt="private search engine" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-32666" height="236" src="https://cdn-resprivacy.pressidium.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/private-search-engine.png" width="459"/></p>
<p style="text-align: left;">Here are a few privacy-friendly search engines worth consideration:</p>
<ol>
<li style="text-align: left;"><a href="https://searx.me" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Searx</a> – A very privacy-friendly and versatile metasearch engine.</li>
<li style="text-align: left;"><a href="https://www.qwant.com/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Qwant</a> – A private search engine based in France.</li>
<li style="text-align: left;"><a href="https://metager.de/en" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Metager</a> – A private search engine based in Germany.</li>
<li style="text-align: left;"><a href="https://duckduckgo.com/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">DuckDuckGo</a> – DDG has a long history of being a great Google search alternative, but they do record search terms and are partners with Yahoo.</li>
<li style="text-align: left;"><a href="https://www.startpage.com/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">StartPage</a> – StartPage gives you Google search results, but without the tracking.</li>
</ol>
<div class="shadow-spacer">
<div class="mask"></div>
</div>
<h3 style="text-align: left;">ENCRYPTED CLOUD STORAGE</h3>
<p style="text-align: left;">Large cloud storage companies, such as DropBox and Google, usually have horrible privacy policies and practices. Here are a few alternatives:</p>
<ol style="text-align: left;">
<li><a href="http://www.teamdrive.com/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">TeamDrive</a> – This is a business-oriented cloud backup and file synchronization option based in Germany.</li>
<li><a href="https://tresorit.com" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Tresorit</a> – This is a user-friendly cloud storage option based in Switzerland. They offer client-side encryption, but also utilize Microsoft Windows servers, which is one drawback.</li>
<li><a href="https://nextcloud.com" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Nextcloud</a> – Nextcloud is an open source, self-hosted file share and communication platform. They are based in Germany.</li>
<li><a href="https://www.sync.com/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Sync.com</a> – Based in Canada, Sync.com offers a secure, encrypted cloud storage solution for businesses and individuals.</li>
</ol>
<p style="text-align: left;">Of course, you can also encrypt you files and store them locally. One good option for this is <a href="https://www.veracrypt.fr/en/Home.html" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">VeraCrypt</a>, which is available for free and supports Windows, Mac OS, and Linux.</p>
<div class="shadow-spacer">
<div class="mask"></div>
</div>
<h3 style="text-align: left;">PASSWORD MANAGER</h3>
<p style="text-align: left;">Using a secure password manager is also an important step for protecting your data. Here are a few good options:</p>
<ol>
<li style="text-align: left;"><a href="https://keepass.info/download.html" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">KeePass</a> – KeePass is a free, open source password manager that keeps all passwords secured in a database that is locked with a master key or key file. Support for: Windows, Mac OS, Linux, iOS, Android, and BSD.</li>
<li style="text-align: left;"><a href="https://lesspass.com/#/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">LessPass</a> – LessPass is a free and open source password manager that will also generate unique passwords. Support for Windows, Mac OS, Linux, and Android.</li>
<li style="text-align: left;"><a href="https://bitwarden.com" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">Bitwarden</a> – Bitwarden is another great open source password manager that is free and easy to use. Bitwarden can be used on all major operating systems and browsers.</li>
</ol>
