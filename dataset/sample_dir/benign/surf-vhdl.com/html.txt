<h2>When FPGAs do not
implement a multiplier HW macro</h2>
<p>The modern
FPGAs implement multiplication using a dedicated hardware resource. Such
dedicated hardware resource generally implements 18×18 multiply and accumulate function.
Many FPGAs use two 9×9 multiplier IP to implement a single 18×18 multiplier
macro. It depends on the technology you are using. </p>
<p>Depending on the technology and FPGA you are using it is also possible to have no multiplier at all as dedicated IP. In this case, if you need to perform a multiplication it could be a problem. Multiplication is a very demanding operator in term of area and timing resources, so you need to pay attention to the operand number of bits in order to minimize the area and timing impact on your FPGA. </p>

<h2>Connecting two devices</h2>
<p>When we need to transfer data
from two different devices, the simple way is to use the minimum numbers of
wires. For instance, if we need to transfer a data bus of 16 bits between two

<div class="entry-meta">
<span class="posted-on"><i class="fa fa-calendar-alt"></i> <a href="https://surf-vhdl.com/how-to-compensate-amplitude-and-phase-imbalance-in-vhdl/" rel="bookmark"><time class="entry-date published" datetime="2019-03-04T22:25:27+01:00">March 4, 2019</time><time class="updated" datetime="2019-03-31T21:05:54+01:00">March 31, 2019</time></a></span><span class="byline"> <i class="fa fa-user"></i> <span class="author vcard"><a class="url fn n" href="https://surf-vhdl.com/author/francesco/">Surf-VHDL</a></span></span> <span class="comments-link"><i class="fa fa-comments"></i><a href="https://surf-vhdl.com/how-to-compensate-amplitude-and-phase-imbalance-in-vhdl/#respond">Leave a comment</a></span>

<div class="entry-content">
<p>The real mixing refers to the translation of a signal in frequency by multiplying it with a single oscillator signal as in figure below</p>
<figure class="wp-block-image"><img alt="" class="wp-image-1877" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px" src="https://surf-vhdl.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/post-iq-modulator-demodulator-1024x537.png" srcset="https://surf-vhdl.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/post-iq-modulator-demodulator-1024x537.png 1024w, https://surf-vhdl.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/post-iq-modulator-demodulator-300x157.png 300w, https://surf-vhdl.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/post-iq-modulator-demodulator-768x403.png 768w, https://surf-vhdl.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/post-iq-modulator-demodulator.png 1200w"/><figcaption>Figure 1 – Typical I/Q modulator-demodulator architecture<a name="Figure1"></a></figcaption></figure>
<p>This approach is performed using the I/Q
modulator technique. The I/Q modulator is a critical component in the signal
chain for modern digital transmitters. An I/Q modulators perform the frequency
translation that mixes the baseband signal to the desired location in the RF
spectrum. An I/Q modulator consists of a local oscillator (LO) input that is
split into in-phase (I) and quadrature (Q) components that are separated by
90°. An example of I/Q modulator is given in [3] These two signals drive separate mixers that
are also driven by I and Q baseband signals processor. </p>

<div class="entry-content">
<p>I wrote many posts on different VHDL
topics. Many of them deal with quite a complex concept.</p>
<p>Some days ago, I realized that I didn’t
write anything “simple” dealing with the basic hardware building block. </p>
<p>I realized that since I received many email
and telegram messages asking me to explain them some line of VHDL code. These
lines are relative to a simple shift register implementation.</p>
<blockquote class="wp-block-quote"><p>Sometimes what is simple for you, is not for other people!</p></blockquote>
<p>In this post, I would like to propose <strong>three different ways</strong> of implementing a shift register in VHDL.</p>
<p>First of all, let’s review what is a shift
register. It could be represented with a series of flip flop connected in
series, where the output of one flip-flop is the input of the other.</p>
<p>In <a href="#Figure1">Figure 1</a> you can see a visual representation.</p>

<div class="entry-content">
<h2>The errors occur in burst</h2>
<p> </p>
<p>When we establish a communication between two or more devices we are prone to errors.</p>
<p><a href="https://surf-vhdl.com/how-to-implement-a-reed-solomon-encoder-in-vhdl/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">In this post</a>, we can see how to prevent errors using a coding technique like Reed-Solomon coding. There are a lot of coding techniques in the literature.</p>
<p>But the only data coding is not enough for a good prevention of communication error.</p>
<p>The encoders/decoders work fine on a “sparse” error condition. In a communication channel errors occur in bursts.</p>
<p>Think about it.</p>
<p>When you have a perturbation over a channel, it persists on the channel for a certain amount of time.</p>
<p>During the perturbation on the communication link, the number of bits involved will be proportional to the speed of the link: higher is the link speed, higher will be the consecutive number of bits that can be affected.</p>
<blockquote><p>So, what could be a solution to this issue?</p></blockquote>

<h2>FIR filter overview</h2>
<p>When we implement an FIR filter in <strong>FPGA or ASIC</strong> the major cost in terms of area resources derives from the multiplier units required for coefficient multiplication. Depending on the frequency response of the filter, the number of coefficients (or filter taps) could become large.</p>

<h2 class="entry-title"><a href="https://surf-vhdl.com/how-to-implement-a-reed-solomon-encoder-in-vhdl/" rel="bookmark">How to implement a Reed-Solomon Encoder in VHDL</a></h2>
<div class="entry-meta">
<span class="posted-on"><i class="fa fa-calendar-alt"></i> <a href="https://surf-vhdl.com/how-to-implement-a-reed-solomon-encoder-in-vhdl/" rel="bookmark"><time class="entry-date published" datetime="2018-07-25T19:16:12+01:00">July 25, 2018</time><time class="updated" datetime="2018-07-25T19:16:12+01:00">July 25, 2018</time></a></span><span class="byline"> <i class="fa fa-user"></i> <span class="author vcard"><a class="url fn n" href="https://surf-vhdl.com/author/francesco/">Surf-VHDL</a></span></span> <span class="comments-link"><i class="fa fa-comments"></i><a href="https://surf-vhdl.com/how-to-implement-a-reed-solomon-encoder-in-vhdl/#respond">Leave a comment</a></span>
</div><!-- .entry-meta -->
</header><!-- .entry-header -->
<div class="entry-content">
<h2>What is an RS-Encoder</h2>
<p> </p>
<p>When you want to transfer information from a source to a target, you want to be sure that such information is transferred without errors.</p>
<p>When you transfer an information through a communication channel, such information will be prone to errors.</p>
<p>In order to minimize or eliminate the number of errors during the information transmission, we can adopt some error correction strategies.</p>
<p></p><figure class="thumbnail wp-caption aligncenter" id="attachment_1708" style="width: 1210px"><img alt="" class="wp-image-1708 size-full" height="629" sizes="(max-width: 1200px) 100vw, 1200px" src="https://surf-vhdl.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/post-satellite-communication.png" srcset="https://surf-vhdl.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/post-satellite-communication.png 1200w, https://surf-vhdl.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/post-satellite-communication-300x157.png 300w, https://surf-vhdl.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/post-satellite-communication-768x403.png 768w, https://surf-vhdl.com/wp/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/post-satellite-communication-1024x537.png 1024w" width="1200"/><figcaption class="caption wp-caption-text">Figure 1 – Satellite communication corrupted by the link channel noise<a name="Figure1"></a></figcaption></figure><p></p>
<p>Such strategies are named <strong>FEC</strong>, i.e. <strong>Forward Error Correction</strong>. As you can imagine, nothing is for free. If you want to guarantee an error-free transmission you need to “pay” in terms of transmission bandwidth and transmitter/receiver complexity.</p>
<p>Any error correction strategy needs to transmit much more information than the minimum required, in order to allow the receiver to recognize if the received information is correct and, in case of error, provide to correct the errors.</p>
<p>Without entering in the error correction field theory, very complex and wide argument, here we want to address one possible error correction algorithm.</p>
<p>There are two main approaches to implement Error- Correction Coding:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Block Coding</strong>: the symbol stream is divided into a block and coded.</li>
<li><strong>Convolution Coding</strong>: convolution operation is applied to the symbol stream.</li>
</ul>
